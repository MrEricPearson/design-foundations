# Tragedy of the Commons and Shared Design Systems
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize | **Arc:** Standalone

**Goal:** After this piece, you will be able to recognize when "everyone's responsible" means no one is — and name an owner before something shared degrades past rescue.

**Trigger:** a shared resource (a design system, a shared doc) is degrading and nobody feels responsible.

**Method:** name an explicit owner for the specific piece that's degrading, even informally. Shared ownership without a name usually means no ownership at all.

**Artifact:** one named owner for one specific piece of shared work.

**Proof:** A shared resource with a named owner has one person to hold accountable when something degrades. One without a named owner has no one. "Everyone's responsible" is functionally equivalent to "no one is" when a specific piece of upkeep needs to happen.

**Don't confuse ownership with authority** — naming an owner gives one person accountability; it doesn't give them the resources, time, or authority to fix it. Ownership is a starting point. The owner still needs the capacity to act, and that's a separate conversation from naming them.

**Try This:** name one shared resource in your current work that's degrading. Name one specific person who should own the specific part that's degrading. Tell them. That's the act.

**What Next:** if the shared resource is a design system or component library degrading due to inconsistent additions, read 134 (Design Debt Concept) to frame the conversation around what's compounding and why. If ownership is contested or unclear at a team level, that's a 304 (Workshop) conversation — it needs the right people in the room to commit.
