# Film Review Culture for Design Critique
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize | **Arc:** Standalone. Pairs with 136 (Critique vs. Feedback) for the mode to set before watching.

**Goal:** After this piece, you will be able to shift a critique session from memory-based approval-seeking to reaction-to-what's-actually-there — using a recording as the shared object.

**Trigger:** critique sessions feel more like approval-seeking than real feedback.

**Method:** record a working session (with agreement from everyone involved) and watch a short clip back together before the next critique — react to the work itself, not memory of it.

**Artifact:** a few minutes of reviewed footage, discussed together.

**Proof:** A reaction to a clip is a reaction to what's actually in it. A reaction to memory is a reaction to what you remember — which has already been filtered by what you noticed at the time and what you've thought since. The clip makes the discussion about the same thing for everyone in the room.

**Don't confuse the clip with the subject of critique** — the clip is an object that makes the reaction shared and observable; the subject of critique is still the design decision, the behavior pattern, or the flow — not the quality of the recording or who appears in it. The film review frame shifts from "what do you think of our work?" to "what do you notice in this clip?" That shift is the whole method.

**Try This:** before your next critique session, record two minutes of yourself or a colleague working through the design as if using it for the first time. Play the clip at the start of the session. Let the reaction be to what's in the clip, not to memory.

**What Next:** if the critique session's problem is that it's generating approval-seeking rather than honest evaluation, read 136 (Critique vs. Feedback) to set the right mode before the session starts. If you want to turn what the clip surfaces into a structured usability finding, read 215a (Moderated Usability Session) for the method that makes it repeatable — or 215b (Unmoderated Usability Testing) if you want participants to work through the design independently.
