# A Discovery Space Separate from Delivery Work
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize (Practice Atom) | **Arc:** Standalone | **Prereqs:** 100 | **Episode:** 2

**Goal:** Recognize when discovery thinking is getting lost because it's happening inside delivery infrastructure — and create a physical separation that preserves it across sessions.

**Concept:** The working assumption is that discovery happens naturally as you work — questions get answered, assumptions get surfaced, and the process of building does the clarifying. The correction: open questions and surface assumptions need a persistent home that isn't inside the work itself. The mechanism: delivery infrastructure (tickets, branches, commits) is organized around settled decisions. Anything that doesn't have an answer yet tends to get resolved or discarded — not because it was addressed, but because it didn't fit the structures designed for resolved things. Questions and assumptions that don't get captured during the session they arise in are almost never revisited — they get absorbed into the direction without resolution, or lost entirely.

A designated discovery space isn't about documentation; it's about preserving the legibility of uncertainty. An open question that's written down and visible is available to be answered or transferred to someone who can answer it. An open question that lived in your head during last Thursday's session has no status at the start of Monday's.

**The move:** Create a folder or space explicitly for discovery — separate from production code or delivery artifacts. Maintain two files in it: open questions (things you don't know yet) and current assumptions (things you're treating as true that you haven't verified). Start each new piece of work by checking both files.

**Don't confuse a discovery space with documentation.** Documentation is for settled decisions: what was decided, why, and for whom. A discovery space is for unresolved thinking — it should look messy. A false positive: a clean, well-organized notes folder feels like a discovery space. If it only contains things that are already resolved, it's documentation that happens to use a notes app. The test is whether it contains open questions you haven't answered yet.

**Try Noticing:** Think about your current work. Where do open questions actually live — in a designated place, in a chat thread, in your head? How many questions from the last session are still accessible and reviewable right now?

**What Next:** Once a discovery space is active, use it as the starting point for 122 (Starting Questions) before the next piece of work begins. When you're ready to move from discovery into structured planning, read 200 (Story Mapping in Cursor).
