# Self-Critique Without Another Person
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize (Practice Atom) | **Arc:** Standalone | **Prereqs:** 103 | **Episode:** 2

**Goal:** Recognize when familiarity is hiding the obvious problems in your own work — and apply the structural move that resets the view.

**Concept:** The working assumption is that being close to your own work makes you the most qualified to evaluate it. The correction: familiarity produces a specific blind spot — your own choices stop appearing as choices and start appearing as the only way the work could have been done. The mechanism is self-reference: when you review your own work immediately after creating it, every decision reads as intentional and logical because you remember the reasoning. The flaws you can't see are almost never the subtle ones; they're the ones that seem obvious to anyone who wasn't inside your reasoning process. Temporal distance breaks that self-reference loop. A few minutes away is enough to make your choices visible as choices again.

**The move:** Step away from the work for at least a few minutes. Return and read it as if it's someone else's work — ask the questions you would ask them: what's confusing here, what's missing, what would I push back on?

**Don't confuse self-critique with editing.** Editing improves what's already there. Self-critique questions whether what's there is right. A false positive: finding a few things to refine after stepping away feels like a successful self-critique. But if all of the notes are about polish (word choice, visual balance, phrasing), and none are structural ("this part doesn't actually address the real problem"), the distance produced editing, not critique. The stranger's-eye question surfaces structural issues, not surface ones.

**Try Noticing:** Look at something you've been working on for more than an hour. What would you ask the author if it weren't yours? Notice where you hesitate — the hesitation marks the questions you're suppressing because you know the reasoning, not because the reasoning is sound.
