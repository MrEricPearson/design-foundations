# Your Derisking Toolkit
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize | **Arc:** The Risk in What You Assume, Part 5 | **Prereqs:** 103 | **Episode:** 1

**Goal:** Recognize which form of risk you're actually facing — so you can reach for the tool that addresses it rather than defaulting to the same approach regardless of what's in front of you.

**Concept:** The working assumption is that derisking is a process — a standard sequence of steps (research, then prototype, then test) that reduces risk when executed in the right order. The correction: risk takes four distinct forms, and each form requires a different response. Applying a single approach to all risk types either imposes unnecessary overhead on low-risk decisions or completely misses the specific risk that's actually present. The mechanism: risk isn't uniform because the thing that makes it risky isn't uniform. Not knowing the shape of what you're building is a different problem from having assumptions you can't see, which is different from not knowing if your approach actually works, which is different from having blind spots that only a different vantage point can surface.

Four tools, each matched to a risk form:
- **Visibility** — when you don't know the shape of what you're building. Makes structure explicit before it's locked in. → *Story Mapping in Cursor*
- **Bias recognition** — when you suspect assumptions are hiding inside the framing. Surfaces what you're assuming without knowing it. → *From a Vague Ask to a Real Persona*
- **Iteration** — when you don't know if your approach works. Tests the riskiest assumption first, adjusts, then tests again. → *Mocking and Testing an Agent Handoff*
- **Co-creation** — when blind spots are the primary risk. Brings in a different vantage point to catch what you can't see alone. → *Running One Workshop, Start to Finish*

These aren't sequential. There's no single right order, and no mandate to run all four. Discovery before delivery, delivery first, or both tangled — all work depending on what risk is actually present.

**You'll see it when:** A team defaults to the same derisking approach for every project regardless of what specifically is risky. Or a team skips derisking entirely because the standard approach doesn't fit the timeline. Both are symptoms of treating derisking as one thing rather than four.

**The signal:** The derisking approach was chosen before the risk type was identified. The team is running research because "we always do research," not because the specific risk calls for it.

**Don't confuse the toolkit with a pipeline.** Running all four tools on every decision is waste. Using none is avoidance. A false positive: a team that does research, builds a prototype, and runs a usability test feels like it's covered all four risk types. But this sequence addresses shape (visibility) and approach validation (iteration) — it doesn't automatically address hidden assumptions or blind spots. The coverage depends on what the specific research methods and test designs were set up to surface, not on whether the sequence was run.

**Try Noticing:** Look at the riskiest assumption in your current work. Can you name which risk form it represents — shape unknown, assumptions hidden, approach unvalidated, or blind spots likely? Now look at what derisking is currently planned. Does it address that specific form?
