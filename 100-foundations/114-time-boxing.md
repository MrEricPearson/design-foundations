# Time-Boxing
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize (Practice Atom) | **Arc:** Standalone | **Prereqs:** 113 | **Episode:** 2

**Goal:** Recognize when open-ended work needs a boundary rather than more time — and apply the constraint that forces a decision instead of deferring it.

**Concept:** The working assumption is that work should continue until it's done — that stopping before completion means stopping before you've gotten the value. The correction: open-ended work expands to fill available time, and more time doesn't always produce better output. The mechanism is that "I'll stop when it's done" is a moving target — the definition of done shifts as work progresses, and the feeling of "almost there" can persist indefinitely. A fixed stopping point converts "I'll stop when it's done" into "I'll stop at X and assess" — two fundamentally different prompts. The assessment at the end of a time box is more honest than one made mid-flow, because the clock removes the option of continuing rather than deciding.

The value of time-boxing isn't efficiency — it's the forced assessment. What you learn in the assessment is what determines whether more time is worth spending, which is a better basis for that decision than the feeling of almost-doneness.

**The move:** Before starting, name a specific time limit. When it expires, stop and complete: "What did I learn, and is the next step clearer than when I started?" Let the assessment determine whether to continue.

**Don't confuse a time box with a deadline.** A deadline is an external constraint that ends with whatever you have. A time box is a deliberate self-imposed boundary that ends with a learning assessment. A false positive: stopping at a fixed time and immediately restarting without an assessment looks like time-boxing and doesn't produce its value. The assessment is the mechanism, not the stopping.

**Try Noticing:** Look at something you're currently working on that could expand indefinitely. What would you assess if you had to stop in 20 minutes? Notice whether that assessment is something you'd actually find out in 20 minutes, or whether you're currently avoiding making it explicit.
