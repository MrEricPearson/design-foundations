# Name What People Do, Not What They Think
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize | **Note:** Foundational prereq for 214 (Affinity Mapping); pairs with 155 (Synthesis vs. Analysis), 151 (Self-Report vs. Observed Behavior)

**Goal:** Recognize when an observation has slipped from describing behavior to interpreting intent — so the data stays recoverable.

**Concept:** What people do is observable. What people think is interpreted. "She clicked the back button three times" is a behavior. "She was confused" is an inference about why. Research notes that conflate the two produce artifacts that look like evidence but contain hidden interpretations — and the original behavior is no longer recoverable from the note.

**You'll see it when:** An affinity map or research note describes a user's state rather than their action. "Users felt frustrated" vs. "Users re-entered their information twice without completing the form." The first note is an interpretation; the second is a behavior that generates the interpretation as a claim, with the evidence still visible.

**The signal:** The note can't be verified against the recording or the transcript. "She was confused" can't be checked — confusion is inferred from behavior. "She said 'wait, what?' and scrolled back to the top" can be checked. If the note can't be verified, it's already been interpreted.

**Don't confuse this with:** Insight — which is a valid interpretation derived from multiple behavior observations and explicitly labeled as such. An insight is a claim: "based on observations across five participants, users appear to rely on visual hierarchy to locate actions rather than reading labels." A raw observation should describe only what happened, not what it means.

**Try Noticing:** Look at the last set of research notes you took or reviewed. Find one note that describes a user state — confused, frustrated, happy, unsure. Rewrite it as the observable behavior that produced that interpretation. What changed? Is the claim still as strong, or was the interpretation doing work that the behavior alone doesn't support?

**What Next:** When you're clustering observations into patterns, read 214 (Affinity Mapping) — the distinction between behavior notes and interpretation notes is the most critical judgment in that method. Mixing the two in the same cluster produces clusters that mix different levels of evidence.
