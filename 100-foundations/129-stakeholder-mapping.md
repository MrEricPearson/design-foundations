# Stakeholder Mapping
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize (Practice Atom) | **Arc:** Standalone | **Prereqs:** none | **Episode:** 14

**Goal:** Recognize who has the ability to affect the outcome of your work — and surface that before stakeholder surprises happen mid-project.

**Concept:** The working assumption is that you know who the stakeholders are — the people you work with directly and the ones who'll review at the end. The correction: stakeholder impact doesn't correlate with visibility. The people most likely to derail a project are often not the ones most present in the work. The mechanism: two variables determine how a stakeholder will affect an outcome — power (the ability to block, redirect, or accelerate) and interest (how closely they're watching and how much they care). These are independent. A stakeholder with high power and low interest won't block your work unless they're surprised by it — a single surprise at the wrong moment can undo months of progress. A stakeholder with low power and high interest won't block your work but will amplify it in either direction. Mapping both dimensions before work begins surfaces who needs what type of engagement.

Four zones: high power + high interest → manage closely, early; high power + low interest → keep satisfied, prevent surprises; low power + high interest → keep informed, they're ambassadors or early critics; low power + low interest → monitor, but a low-interest stakeholder can become high-interest unexpectedly.

**The move:** Write every person or group who touches or is affected by the work. Place each in a zone. Treat the placements as assumptions — revisit when the situation changes.

**Don't confuse this with a communications plan.** The map tells you who matters and in what way. A communications plan tells you what to tell them. The map comes first. A false positive: a stakeholder list that includes everyone on the project team feels comprehensive. But it usually captures people with interest and misses people with power who aren't in the daily work. The missing person in "high power, low interest" is the one that typically produces the mid-project surprise.

**Try Noticing:** Think of a piece of work that was delayed or redirected by a stakeholder surprise. Where would that person have landed on the power/interest map? Was there anything in place that would have flagged them as high-power before the surprise?
