# Pattern vs. Data Point
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize | **Note:** Foundational for 202 arc, 214 (Affinity Mapping); pairs with 149

**Goal:** Recognize the difference between a single observation and a pattern — so you know when findings justify action and when they justify more investigation.

**Concept:** A data point is a single instance of something: one user who struggled, one response that surprised you, one observation that contradicts your assumption. A pattern is the same thing appearing across multiple independent instances — not just more data, but data from different people in different contexts that converges on the same thing. Data points are signals. Patterns are findings. A finding supports a decision. A signal tells you where to look.

**You'll see it when:** One user in a usability session can't find the navigation, and the team concludes the navigation is broken. Or: one interview surfaces a frustration nobody expected, and the team immediately routes to solutions. Both conclusions may be correct — but at one instance, they're hypotheses, not findings.

**The signal:** Ask how many independent instances support the claim. Independent means from different people who didn't coordinate their answers, not the same person saying the same thing twice. A pattern usually requires at least three or four independent instances before it starts to be reliable, and more for decisions with high stakes. The number isn't magic — the question is whether the instances are independent and whether they converge consistently.

**Don't confuse this with:** The argument that you need large sample sizes to act. Small numbers of data points can be enough to act when the stakes are low, when the data is behavioral (observed) rather than self-reported, or when even one instance signals a risk that justifies fixing. The issue isn't sample size as a rule — it's knowing when you have a finding versus a signal, and treating them accordingly.

**Try Noticing:** Think of the last finding that drove a design decision. How many independent instances supported it? If the answer is "one" or "a few," that's a signal — and "signal" means investigate further before committing. If the answer is "consistently across multiple people," that's a pattern and more likely to be actionable.
