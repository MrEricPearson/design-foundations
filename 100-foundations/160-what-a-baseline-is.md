# What a Baseline Is
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize | **Note:** Soft prereq for 217 (UX Metrics); pairs with 113 (Defining Success Before You Start)

**Goal:** Recognize when a measurement has a baseline and when it doesn't — because a number without a before-state can't tell you whether anything changed.

**Concept:** A baseline is the measurement of a thing before an intervention. It's the starting point that makes an after-measurement meaningful. Without a baseline, you can measure a current state but you cannot determine whether it improved, worsened, or stayed the same. A task completion rate of 72% is not, by itself, good or bad — it depends on whether it was 58% before the redesign (good) or 89% (a regression). The baseline is what converts a measurement from a snapshot into a signal.

**You'll see it when:** A team ships a redesign and then points to a positive metric — "NPS is 38!" — without knowing what NPS was before. Or a UX improvement is presented at a review with current numbers but no comparison point. The numbers are real; the improvement is asserted, not demonstrated.

**The signal:** Ask "what was the number before?" If no one knows, there's no baseline, and the current measurement tells you about the current state only — not about whether anything changed. The absence of a baseline is often invisible because the current number seems meaningful on its own.

**Don't confuse this with:** The need for a perfect historical baseline before you can start measuring. You can establish a baseline now — measure the current state before you make a change — and then measure again after. The baseline doesn't have to precede everything; it has to precede the thing you're trying to evaluate. Starting now is always better than waiting for a complete historical record.

**Try Noticing:** Think of one metric that was cited recently as evidence that something worked. Was there a comparison point — a before-state — cited alongside it? If not, that metric is a snapshot, not a proof of change. Notice how often improvement claims are made without a stated baseline.

**What Next:** When defining a metric before you build, read 217 (UX Metrics) for the HEART framework and how to write a metric with its category, baseline, and target before the work starts.
