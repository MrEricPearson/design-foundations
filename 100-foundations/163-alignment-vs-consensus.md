# Alignment vs. Consensus
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize | **Note:** Foundational prereq for 304 (Running One Workshop); pairs with 164 (Generative vs. Convergent Thinking)

**Goal:** Recognize the difference between alignment and consensus — so you know which one a session needs to produce and stop expecting the wrong outcome.

**Concept:** Alignment means everyone understands the direction and can act consistently with it, even if they'd have chosen differently. Consensus means everyone agrees it's the right direction. A workshop can produce alignment without consensus. Confusing the two leads to reopening decisions that were already made — because the session achieved one outcome while everyone expected the other.

**You'll see it when:** A team leaves a meeting having agreed on a direction, then someone who was in the room keeps advocating for a different approach. The session produced alignment — everyone knew the direction — but not consensus — not everyone believed it was right. Because the type of agreement wasn't named, the reopening looks like bad faith when it's actually just an unmet expectation.

**The signal:** If alignment was achieved, people can accurately describe the direction even if they'd have chosen differently. If consensus was achieved, people believe the direction is right, not just aware of it. Ask: "could someone who disagrees with this direction still execute it correctly?" Alignment says yes. Consensus says they'd want to.

**Don't confuse this with:** Buy-in — which is closer to consensus but specifically refers to commitment to execute. You can have buy-in without genuine agreement ("I disagree but I'll support it"). Alignment doesn't require buy-in; it only requires shared understanding.

**Try Noticing:** Think of a decision you've seen reopen after it was "agreed" in a meeting. Was the outcome alignment or consensus? Which one was the session designed to produce? If no one named it, that gap is likely where the reopening came from.

**What Next:** If you're designing a workshop and need to decide which outcome to build toward, read 304 (Running One Workshop) for the facilitation mechanics that serve each outcome differently.
