# Exploratory vs. Explanatory Visualization
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize | **Note:** Soft prereq for 212 (Data Visualization); pairs with 212

**Goal:** Recognize whether a data visualization is for exploring data to find insights or for explaining a conclusion to an audience — because the two require completely different design choices.

**Concept:** Exploratory visualization is built for the analyst: interactive, detail-rich, designed to surface patterns that aren't yet known. Explanatory visualization is built for the audience: focused, annotated, designed to make one insight legible to someone who doesn't have time to explore. A visualization built for exploration shown to an executive produces confusion; a visualization built for explanation used by an analyst misses everything interesting. The question "who is this for, and what do they already know?" determines which type you're building.

**You'll see it when:** A chart built for internal analysis gets shared in a presentation without modification — and the audience can't tell what it's saying because it shows everything, including the noise. Or a presentation-ready chart gets used for data analysis — and the analyst can't find anything because all the detail has been removed.

**The signal:** If the visualization requires the viewer to interact with it or explore it to find the point, it's exploratory. If the visualization makes one specific point immediately visible to someone seeing it for the first time, it's explanatory. Neither is better — they serve different moments in the same workflow.

**Don't confuse this with:** Simple vs. complex — an explanatory visualization can be complex if the conclusion it's illustrating is complex. An exploratory visualization can be visually simple while still being analytically rich. The distinction is about purpose and audience, not about visual density.

**Try Noticing:** Think of the last chart you built or shared. Who was the intended audience, and what were they supposed to do with it? Did the chart's design match that purpose — was the right amount of detail visible for the right use? The mismatch between chart purpose and chart design is the gap this concept reveals.
