# Empathy as Cognitive Simulation
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize | **Note:** Foundational prereq for 301 (From Vague Ask to Real Persona) and 301c (Assumption-First Proto-Persona)

**Goal:** Recognize what empathy means as a design practice — cognitive simulation — so it can be applied as a skill rather than invoked as a sentiment.

**Concept:** Empathy in design is cognitive simulation: using what you know about a person to reason about what they experience, expect, and need. It's not the feeling of caring about users. It's not self-referential ("I would feel X in this situation"). It's a structured inference: given what I know about this person's context, goals, and constraints, what is this experience likely to feel like from the inside?

**You'll see it when:** A team makes a design decision by reasoning from the user's perspective — not from what looks good to designers or how it feels personally, but from what the user is trying to accomplish, what they expect, and where the design will break that expectation. The reasoning starts with the user, not with the team's response.

**The signal:** The reasoning explicitly names the user's goal and context before drawing any conclusion about the design. "Given that they're trying to do X under constraint Y, they'll expect Z here" — that's cognitive simulation. "I think this feels right" is not.

**Don't confuse this with:** Sympathy (feeling for someone) or user research (gathering evidence). Empathy as a design skill is applied between research sessions — it uses what you've learned to fill in gaps with principled simulation rather than guesswork. Simulation without research as its foundation produces confident-feeling guesses. Research without simulation applied afterward produces data without inference.

**Try Noticing:** The next time you make or witness a design decision, ask: where does the reasoning start? If it starts with the user's context and goals and reasons forward to the design, that's cognitive simulation. If it starts with the design and reasons backward to a user who might like it, something else is happening.

**What Next:** When you're building a persona to capture what you know about a user for ongoing reference, read 301 (From Vague Ask to Real Persona) for the discovery-driven approach, or 301c (Assumption-First Proto-Persona) for the assumption-first approach when research access isn't yet available.
