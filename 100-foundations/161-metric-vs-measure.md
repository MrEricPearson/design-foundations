# Metric vs. Measure
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize | **Note:** Soft prereq for 217 (UX Metrics); pairs with 160 (What a Baseline Is) and 113 (Defining Success Before You Start)

**Goal:** Recognize the difference between a number you can track and a number connected to a success criterion — so you can tell when you're measuring something meaningful versus just measuring.

**Concept:** A measure is any quantity you can count or calculate: page views, session duration, error rate, NPS score, number of clicks. These exist independent of whether they're connected to a goal. A metric is a measure with a success criterion attached to it: a defined direction (higher is better, or lower is better), a target value, and a connection to something you're trying to achieve. You can track a measure indefinitely without learning anything about performance. A metric tells you whether you're on track toward a specific goal. The difference is whether someone asked "what does success look like?" before deciding what to count.

**You'll see it when:** A dashboard full of numbers is presented as evidence that design work is being tracked, but no number has a target attached to it. Everything is being measured. Nothing is being evaluated. The numbers move and no one can say with confidence whether the movement is good or bad.

**The signal:** For any tracked number, ask: "What's the target, and what direction indicates success?" A measure with no answer to those questions is a number that's tracked for the sake of tracking. A metric is a number where someone can immediately say: "We're aiming for X, and up/down means we're getting there." That specificity is what converts a measure into actionable information.

**Don't confuse this with:** The claim that tracking anything is useless without a predefined target. Exploratory measurement — tracking things to understand the landscape before defining goals — is legitimate. The distinction matters when a number is being used to evaluate whether something worked. For evaluation, you need a metric. For exploration, a measure is fine.

**Try Noticing:** Look at the last report or dashboard you reviewed. For each number shown, ask whether a target was defined before the work started. If the target was defined after the work was done, you're looking at retroactive metric definition — where the number is already known and the success criterion is chosen to fit it. That's a different (and less reliable) thing than defining success before measuring it.

**What Next:** When you're ready to connect a design change to a measurable outcome, read 217 (UX Metrics) for the HEART framework — each HEART category gives a direction and a target type, making it easier to define a metric rather than just a measure.
