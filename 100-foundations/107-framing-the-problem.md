# Framing the Problem
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize (Practice Atom) | **Arc:** Standalone | **Prereqs:** 100 | **Episode:** 2

**Goal:** Recognize when a stated problem is actually a stated solution — and write the sentence that separates what you're solving from how you're solving it.

**Concept:** The working assumption is that a vague ask becomes clearer through building — you'll figure out what you're really solving as you go. The correction: building on an unframed problem doesn't clarify the problem; it embeds the solution assumptions into the work, making them progressively harder to question. The mechanism: when a problem is stated as a solution ("build a dashboard," "add a filter," "redesign the onboarding flow"), the actual user problem that motivated it stays invisible. Solutions designed against invisible problems satisfy the stated requirement and may miss the actual need entirely. Worse, the solution becomes the de facto frame for all subsequent decisions — scope, priority, success criteria — even when those decisions should be grounded in the problem, not the solution.

The template that forces separation: "[Who] is struggling to [do what], because [why]." Filling in all three blanks is only possible when the problem is framed as a problem. Filling in "build a dashboard" for "what" immediately shows you've named a solution.

**The move:** Take the current ask, ticket, or brief and rewrite it as "[Who] is struggling to [do what], because [why]." If any blank stops you, that's your actual next step — not building.

**Don't confuse a problem statement with a solution.** If your sentence names a feature, an action, or a deliverable in any of the three blanks, rewrite the "what" as a struggle. A false positive: a sentence that fills all three blanks feels complete. But "[Who] is struggling to use our current dashboard, because it doesn't have the right filters" has a solution embedded in the "because" — "the right filters" is a design assumption, not a cause. The diagnosis goes in the "because" slot, not a preferred solution.

**Try Noticing:** Take the last ticket, ask, or brief you worked from. Can you write it as "[Who] is struggling to [do what], because [why]?" Notice which blank is hardest to fill — that's the gap in the current framing.

**What Next:** If the "because" isn't clear, read 108 (Five Whys) to trace deeper. When you're ready to turn the problem statement into a design question, read 128 (How Might We).
