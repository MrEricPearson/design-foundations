## Goal

Recognize why trusted is the target quality level — and what distinguishes a trusted product from a merely usable one.

## Concept

"Delight" in design vocabulary does not mean animations, personality, or visual appeal. It means a behavioral state: the user has built their workflow around this product and would actively resist losing it. Trusted products are delightful not because they feel good but because they have become load-bearing — the user relies on them without thinking about them.

Usability asks whether a user can complete a task. Trust asks whether the user reaches for this product when the task arises. The gap between them is the difference between a product people use and a product people keep. In professional software, trust is the only quality level that generates real adoption, advocacy, and retention — outcomes that justify the investment and make the product irreplaceable rather than merely acceptable.

## You'll See It When

A product passes usability testing but adoption remains low. Or users find workarounds rather than using the designated feature. Or the product generates no complaints — but also no champions. Silence is not evidence of trust; it is often evidence of indifference.

## The Signal

Users who trust a product describe what they accomplish with it, not the tool itself. Users at the usable level describe the steps. Users at the functional level describe the problems. When feedback shifts from process ("it took me three clicks to find it") to outcome ("I got this done in half the time"), the trusted threshold is nearby.

## Don't Confuse This With

**Perfection.** Trusted products have bugs, constraints, and known workarounds — but their core value is reliable enough that users build behavior around it rather than around its absence. Trust is not the absence of problems; it is the presence of a relationship the user would work to preserve.

## Try Noticing

Think about a recent piece of user feedback — a complaint, a request, or a survey response. Was the person describing what the product doesn't do (functional gap), how hard something is (usability gap), or what they can't count on it for (trust gap)? The layer they're naming tells you exactly which threshold you're working on next.
