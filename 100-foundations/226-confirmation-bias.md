# Confirmation Bias
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize | **Arc:** Standalone | **Prereqs:** 101, 225 | **Episode:** 3

**Goal:** Recognize when research is structured or interpreted in ways that preferentially surface evidence supporting an existing direction.

**Concept:** The working assumption is that careful researchers stay neutral. The correction: confirmation bias is not a character flaw that careful researchers avoid — it is a feature of human cognition that operates in every researcher at every stage of a project. Neutrality cannot be achieved through good intentions; it requires structural design of the research process that works against the bias rather than relying on the researcher to override it.

The mechanism: confirmation bias operates at every stage. In question design, leading questions prime participants toward expected answers. In participant selection, recruiting from existing customers preferentially surfaces people who already fit the model. In observation, attention and note-taking are drawn to moments that confirm the hypothesis; contradictions are coded as participant error or edge cases. In synthesis, confirming observations become evidence; complicating observations become qualifications. The researcher experiencing confirmation bias experiences themselves as thorough and fair — the bias is not visible from the inside.

This is not about research quality or rigor. A thorough researcher with excellent methods and detailed notes can still produce findings saturated with confirmation bias if the research process wasn't designed to surface disconfirming evidence.

**You'll see it when:** Research findings align tightly with decisions that were already in motion; when participants who produced difficult findings are frequently excluded from synthesis; when the research question was formed as a yes/no ("does this work?") rather than generative ("what would have to be true for this to work, and what would make it fail?").

**The signal:** The research question was formed after the design direction was chosen, rather than before.

**Don't confuse this with:** The absence of contradictory findings meaning no problems exist. Confirmation bias doesn't produce evidence that something works — it produces the absence of evidence that it doesn't. These are not the same thing. A study with no disconfirming findings is strong evidence the study wasn't designed to surface them, not strong evidence the design is sound. The false positive: a research report with rich quotes and thick participant notes reads as rigorous. But if the synthesis process preferentially coded supporting observations, the density of the documentation doesn't correct for the selection bias in what got documented.

**Try Noticing:** Review the last research effort on your current project. When was the research question finalized — before or after the design direction was established? Were any participants excluded from synthesis, and for what stated reason? Read the three most inconvenient findings from that research: do they appear in the synthesis output, and if so, how were they resolved?
