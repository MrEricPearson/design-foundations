# Why You Don't Help During Testing
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize | **Note:** Foundational prereq for 215a (Moderated Usability Session) and 215b (Unmoderated Usability Testing)

**Goal:** Recognize why intervening during a usability session destroys its data — and what to do with the discomfort of watching someone struggle.

**Concept:** A usability test measures whether the design supports the user without help. When you intervene — clarify, redirect, reassure — you replace the design with your mediation. The test then records "the design works fine with a designer present," which is not useful information. The moment you help is the moment the data stops being about the design.

**You'll see it when:** A participant is confused, stuck, or frustrated during a session, and the person running it wants to explain, clarify, or redirect them. The instinct to help is accurate — something is wrong. But naming what's wrong or showing how to proceed removes the evidence about what specifically is wrong.

**The signal:** The urge to say "what you want to do here is..." or "try clicking over there" is the signal. That urge confirms a design problem exists. Acting on it conceals what the problem is, which was the thing the session was designed to surface.

**Don't confuse this with:** Asking the participant to keep thinking aloud, which is not intervention — it's a facilitation move that prompts narration without providing information or direction. "Can you tell me what you're thinking?" is allowed and valuable. "You can click that button to move forward" is not.

**Try Noticing:** Think of a time you watched someone use something you built and felt the urge to explain or redirect. The moment of that urge is the exact moment a usability test would record the design's failure. What was the participant doing when the urge hit? That behavior is the finding.

**What Next:** When you're ready to run a usability session, read 215a (Moderated Usability Session). If you're planning an unmoderated session — where intervention isn't possible by design — read 215b (Unmoderated Usability Testing).
