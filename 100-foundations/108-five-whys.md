# Five Whys
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize (Practice Atom) | **Arc:** Standalone | **Prereqs:** 107 | **Episode:** 2

**Goal:** Recognize when you're addressing a symptom instead of a cause — and trace from the visible problem to something you can actually act on.

**Concept:** The working assumption is that the first explanation for a problem is the problem. The correction: visible problems are almost always symptoms; the cause that produced the symptom is one or more levels below what's observable. The mechanism: addressing a symptom produces a solution that works until the symptom recurs — because the underlying cause hasn't changed. The reason this is hard to see: the symptom is the thing that's urgent and in front of you. The cause is invisible because it's what produced the symptom. Following the chain downward ("why did this happen? → why did that happen?") surfaces the level at which an intervention would actually change the output, rather than just managing its visible expression.

Five is a convention, not a target. Stop when you reach something you can do differently — not when you've counted to five.

**The move:** Write the problem. Ask "why did this happen?" Write the answer. Ask "why?" again about that answer. Continue until the answer is something you could actually change — a decision, a process, a resource, a design choice.

**Don't confuse reaching five answers with reaching the cause.** The goal is an actionable level, not a count. A false positive: a chain that gets more abstract as it goes ("→ lack of process → cultural issue → org priorities") feels like depth but is moving away from something actionable, not toward it. The right direction is toward something specific and changeable, even if it's uncomfortable to name.

**Try Noticing:** Take one problem you're currently facing. Ask "why" about it, then "why" about that answer. After three iterations, ask: is this answer something I could actually change? Notice whether the chain is moving toward specificity and actionability or toward abstraction.

**What Next:** If the root cause points to an unclear problem framing, read 107 (Framing the Problem). If you want to stress-test whether your solution will address the root cause before you build, read 207 (Premortem).
