# The Interpretation Gap
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize | **Note:** Foundational prereq for 303 (One Feature, Three Handoffs)

**Goal:** Recognize where interpretation diverges from intention in a handoff — so you can close the gap before it becomes a delivery problem.

**Concept:** The interpretation gap is the difference between what was meant and what was understood at a handoff point. It exists in every handoff between roles — PM to designer, designer to developer, developer to QA — and it's usually invisible because everyone assumes they understood correctly. The gap doesn't produce a wrong answer; it produces a confidently delivered answer to a slightly different question than the one that was asked.

**You'll see it when:** A delivered feature technically matches the specification but doesn't match what the requester intended. Or a question comes back mid-sprint that should have been answerable from the written brief. Both are signs that the handoff contained more interpretive space than anyone noticed at the time it was written.

**The signal:** A delivery required verbal clarification beyond what was written. If the written handoff was sufficient, there was no gap. If someone had to explain in conversation what was meant — after reading the written version — the written handoff had interpretive space that closed differently on each side.

**Don't confuse this with:** Ambiguity — which is a requirement that can't be specified more clearly because the underlying decision hasn't been made. The interpretation gap is the distance between what was written and what was understood from what was written. Both are resolvable, but differently: ambiguity requires a decision; interpretation gaps require clearer communication of the same decision.

**Try Noticing:** Think of the last brief, ticket, or spec you received or gave. Was anything delivered or interpreted differently from what was intended? The divergence is the interpretation gap. Where in the written document did the gap live — what did the writing leave open that it should have closed?

**What Next:** The method for catching interpretation gaps in handoffs before they become delivery problems is 303 (One Feature, Three Handoffs).
