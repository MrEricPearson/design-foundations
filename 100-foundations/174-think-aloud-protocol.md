# Think-Aloud Protocol
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize | **Note:** Foundational prereq for 215a (Moderated Usability Session); pairs with 157 (Why You Don't Help During Testing)

**Goal:** Recognize what the think-aloud protocol is and why it produces evidence about user reasoning that silent observation cannot.

**Concept:** The think-aloud protocol is asking a participant to say what they're thinking while they use something, in real time. It produces evidence about the user's mental model — what they're trying to do, what they're expecting, what's confusing them — that behavioral observation alone doesn't capture. Behavior shows what happened; think-aloud captures why.

**You'll see it when:** A participant narrates while navigating: "I'm looking for the save button — I'd expect it at the top... I'm not sure what this label means... I thought clicking this would take me to the summary." That narration is think-aloud data. A participant who silently clicks through produces behavioral data only — what they did, not what they expected.

**The signal:** The participant's words reference expectations, not just actions. "I thought this would..." or "I expected to see..." or "I'm not sure what this means" — these are think-aloud outputs. If the participant is only describing actions ("I'm clicking here, now I'm scrolling"), the protocol isn't producing mental model data yet.

**Don't confuse this with:** Interview responses, which are given after the fact from memory and shaped by the desire to appear reasonable. Think-aloud is concurrent with the action — it captures the moment of confusion before the participant rationalizes it into a more coherent-sounding retrospective account.

**Try Noticing:** In any session where you're watching someone use something, notice whether you're getting behavioral data (what they do) or mental model data (what they expect and why). Think-aloud converts the second type from invisible to observable. Without it, you see what went wrong; with it, you often see why.

**What Next:** When you're ready to run a moderated usability session using the think-aloud protocol, read 215a (Moderated Usability Session). The primary facilitation prompt is "can you tell me what you're thinking?" — and 157 (Why You Don't Help During Testing) explains the line between this prompt and intervention.
