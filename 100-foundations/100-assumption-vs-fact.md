# Assumption vs. Fact
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize (Practice Atom) | **Arc:** The Risk in What You Assume, Part 1 | **Prereqs:** none | **Episode:** 1

**Goal:** Recognize when a claim driving work is being treated as fact without having been verified — so you can make the invisible visible before building on it.

**Concept:** The working assumption is that a claim needs to be proven false before you question it. The correction: in decision-making, a claim carries the weight of a fact the moment no one has labeled it otherwise. The mechanism is social, not epistemic — once a claim appears in a requirement, ticket, or meeting summary without a flag, it inherits the credibility of the document it's in. Nobody intends to treat it as fact; it just stops being questioned because the window for questioning it feels like it has passed.

Labeling is what creates the distinction — not the underlying evidence. "Assuming: users need to do this in under 60 seconds" is visible, questionable, and testable. "Users need to do this in under 60 seconds" appears as a requirement and becomes a constraint.

**The move:** For each live claim driving current work, write "Assuming:" or "Fact:" before it. For any claim you label "Fact," trace it to its source: who observed it, what data reflects it, or what direct statement supports it. Any claim you can't trace gets re-labeled "Assuming."

**Don't confuse this with forming a hypothesis.** A hypothesis is an assumption you've decided to test. This is about surfacing assumptions you haven't yet noticed or labeled — including ones you won't test. A labeled assumption you're not testing is fine; it's visible. An unlabeled one you're building on is the problem. A false positive: writing "Assuming:" next to a claim feels like you've addressed it. You haven't. Labeling makes the assumption questionable. It doesn't make it less risky until someone acts on that visibility.

**Try Noticing:** Look at the requirements, tickets, or stated goals driving your current work. For each major claim, ask: can I trace this to something observed or directly stated, or did it arrive as inherited context? Count how many you'd have to label "Assuming" rather than "Fact."

**What Next:** Now that you can label assumptions, read 101 (Not All Assumptions Are Equal) to learn which ones are worth stopping for — and which are safe to move with.
