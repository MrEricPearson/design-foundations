# Synthesis vs. Analysis
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize | **Note:** Foundational prereq for 214 (Affinity Mapping); pairs with 156 (Name What People Do, Not What They Think)

**Goal:** Recognize the difference between analysis and synthesis so you know which operation is happening at each step — and can tell when data has been organized without yet being made useful.

**Concept:** Analysis breaks something into parts to understand what it's made of. Synthesis takes parts and finds what they mean together — the pattern, the insight, the claim that holds the parts in a way they don't hold individually. Research generates data that analysis can organize; synthesis is what turns organized data into something that changes a decision.

**You'll see it when:** A research readout lists every theme from every interview but draws no conclusions. That's analysis — organized, but not yet useful. A synthesis output names what the themes reveal together: a claim about why, a pattern that predicts something, a finding that changes what the team should do next.

**The signal:** A synthesis output has fewer items than the data it was derived from, and each item explains something that individual data points don't explain on their own. If the output is just a list of what was said, it's still at the analysis stage — no matter how well organized.

**Don't confuse this with:** Summarization — which compresses data without adding meaning. Synthesis generates a claim from evidence; it arrives at something that wasn't explicitly stated in the data. "Participants described difficulty with the navigation" is a summary. "Participants don't know which navigation item owns checkout-related tasks" is a synthesized claim.

**Try Noticing:** Look at the last research output or notes document you worked with. Find a section that makes a claim — "users don't understand X because Y." That's synthesis. Find a section that lists what was said without drawing a conclusion. That's still analysis. Both are necessary; the question is whether the work stopped at analysis.

**What Next:** The primary method for moving from analysis to synthesis is 214 (Affinity Mapping). If you're deciding whether your research data is ready to drive a decision, ask: has it been synthesized, or only analyzed?
