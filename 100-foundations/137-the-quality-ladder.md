## Goal

Recognize which quality level a product or feature is currently operating at.

## Concept

User experience exists at four quality levels — functional, usable, efficient, and trusted — and each level is a threshold, not a point on a spectrum. Crossing each one unlocks a different user behavior: use, independent adoption, routine, and advocacy. Most products ship functional. Fewer reach usable. Fewer still reach efficient. Trusted is rare — and it is the level at which users build their work around the product by choice rather than necessity.

The thresholds are not additive. A product can add features indefinitely and not cross a single one. Each threshold is crossed by removing a specific kind of friction: capability gaps block functional, comprehension gaps block usable, attention overhead blocks efficient, reliability failures block trusted.

## You'll See It When

A product works correctly but nobody uses it voluntarily. Or users complete tasks but always with visible effort, workarounds, or occasional help-seeking. Or a product is described as "fine" — neither complained about nor defended.

## The Signal

Ask: what would happen to this team's work if this product disappeared tomorrow?

- Functional → minor inconvenience. They'd find another way quickly.
- Usable → moderate friction. They'd need time to adapt.
- Efficient → workflow disruption. The product has become part of how they work.
- Trusted → resistance, advocacy, and sometimes anger. The product is load-bearing.

## Don't Confuse This With

**"Higher quality means more features."** Quality level describes the user's relationship with what already exists — not the product's capability set. A product with twenty features operating at the functional level serves users less well than a three-feature product operating at trusted. Adding features does not cross thresholds. Reducing friction does.

## Try Noticing

Pick one tool you use every day by choice, and one you use because it was assigned. What would change in your work if each disappeared? The difference in your reaction is the quality ladder in practice.
