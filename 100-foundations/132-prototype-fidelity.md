# Prototype Fidelity
**Tier:** 100 — Recognize | **Arc:** Standalone | **Prereqs:** 106 | **Episode:** 13

**Goal:** Recognize when prototype fidelity is mismatched to the question being tested — so you can stop making things more polished than the question requires.

**Concept:** The working assumption is that fidelity is about production quality — how finished something looks, and how much time it took to make. The correction: fidelity is about what question the prototype is designed to answer, independent of how it was produced. The mechanism: a polished prototype sends a social signal that the direction is settled. Reviewers and participants read visual polish as confidence in the decision — which makes them less likely to challenge the underlying direction and more likely to focus on the details of the execution. A rough prototype sends the opposite signal: the direction is still open. That signal is what makes early feedback actionable rather than cosmetic. The tool used to produce a prototype doesn't determine its fidelity — a description of what you want fed to an AI produces high-polish output that can still be testing a rough conceptual question. Roughness is a deliberate communication choice, not a production constraint.

Three fidelity levels, matched to three question types:
- **Conceptual** (does this direction make sense?): a sketch, boxes with labels, a paragraph. Stick figures are appropriate.
- **Navigational** (can someone find what they need?): wireframes or a click-through without final visual design. Structure and labels matter; color and polish don't.
- **Visual/interactive** (does this feel like a finished product?): final design, real copy, working interactions.

**You'll see it when:** A prototype review produces feedback on button color and icon style when the question was about flow logic. Or participants in a usability session treat a polished AI-generated screen as final when the underlying navigation hasn't been validated.

**The signal:** Feedback addresses details rather than the question the prototype was designed to answer. The fidelity was higher than the question required.

**Don't confuse fidelity with polish.** A high-polish prototype testing a conceptual question is low fidelity for its purpose — the polish is mismatched. A rough sketch testing visual hierarchy is high fidelity for its purpose. A false positive: an AI-generated screen that looks finished produces feedback on the visual design even when the session was set up to test navigation. The tool produced polish quickly; the question didn't require it.

**Try Noticing:** Look at the last prototype or mockup you produced. Write the question it was designed to answer. Was the fidelity appropriate for that question? If it was higher than needed, what prompted the extra polish?
