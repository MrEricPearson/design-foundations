Here's a high-level framework we can use to build this out together:

**1. Atomic Content Taxonomy**
Define your "atomic units" — the smallest standalone idea that still delivers value (a technique, a heuristic, a common mistake, a mental model). Group these under your core pillars (UX Research, UI Design, Design Thinking, Design Strategy, PM/Dev intersections). This taxonomy becomes your content backbone and helps spot gaps/overlaps.

**2. Idea Bank & Capture System**
A running, tagged repository of atomic ideas as they occur to you — from work, reading, or conversations. Tag by pillar, format potential, and audience (designers vs. PMs vs. devs). This decouples *idea capture* from *writing*, so you're never starting from a blank page.

**3. Format Library**
A set of repeatable article "shapes" atomic ideas can slot into — e.g., "The Myth of X," "3 Signals That...," "A Framework for...," "Before/After," "Glossary Deep-Dive." Matching idea → format speeds up drafting and creates recognizable series.

**4. Voice & POV Definition**
A short doc defining your expert angle — what makes *your* take on UX distinct (e.g., your platform/DSO leadership lens, enterprise scale, cross-functional bridge-building). This keeps articles cohesive across topics and formats.

**5. Publishing Cadence & Channel Strategy**
Decide where these live (LinkedIn, Medium, personal blog, Substack) and at what rhythm (weekly atomic post vs. biweekly deeper piece). Cadence should match how atomic your content is — smaller units support higher frequency.

**6. Production Pipeline**
A lightweight workflow: Idea → Outline → Draft → Edit → Publish → Repurpose. This is where I'd plug in most directly — helping outline, draft, and tighten pieces once you've selected an atomic idea.

**7. Feedback & Iteration Loop**
Track what resonates (topic, format, pillar) to refine the taxonomy and idea bank over time — atomic content should compound, not just accumulate.

**Bias Is Just an Assumption You Don't Know You're Making**

## Progressive Disclosure and the Risk of Showing Too Much at Once

**Trigger:** a screen or flow is trying to show everything at once, and it's starting to feel overwhelming — for you to build or for someone to use.

**Method:**
1. Identify what someone actually needs in order to make their *next* decision — not everything they might eventually need.
2. Show only that. Everything else waits until it's relevant.
3. Reveal the rest progressively, as they move forward, rather than front-loading it all.

**What you end up with:** one screen or step with something deliberately held back, on purpose, until it's needed.

**Watchout:** hiding too much creates its own risk — people can't act on what they can't see. This isn't about minimalism for its own sake; if someone needs to know something to trust or use what's in front of them, that's not a good candidate to hide.
