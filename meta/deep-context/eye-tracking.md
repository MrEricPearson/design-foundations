# Deep Context: Eye Tracking
**Type:** Deep Context awareness piece | **Not a method piece** — no steps, no how-to | **Not published as standalone content**

**What it is:** Eye tracking is a research method that records exactly where a person's eyes move across a screen — what they look at first, how long they fixate on each element, what they skip entirely, and what path their gaze takes through an interface.

**What it reveals that simpler methods can't:** You can ask users what they noticed, but they can't accurately self-report their own attention. Eye tracking captures pre-conscious attention — where the eye goes before the person decides to read or interact. It produces heat maps (where attention concentrates) and gaze paths (the sequence of fixations), which reveal whether visual hierarchy is actually guiding attention as intended, what elements users ignore entirely despite their visual prominence, and where attention falls before a user makes a decision.

**What you'll need to run it:** Dedicated hardware (mounted eye trackers cost $8,000–$20,000; screen-based trackers $1,000–$5,000) or a remote eye tracking service; a controlled study environment; a researcher to design the study, recruit participants, and interpret the data. Eye tracking studies typically require 15–30 participants for reliable pattern data.

**What atoms in this library give you the concept:** 133 (Visual Hierarchy) — the conceptual model eye tracking empirically measures. If you understand visual hierarchy as a theory, eye tracking gives you ground truth data on whether your hierarchy works as intended in practice.

**When to bring it up with a specialist:** when you have a high-stakes interface element (a navigation pattern, a critical call to action, a dashboard) where you have conflicting hypotheses about attention and the stakes justify empirical measurement. "I want to know if users see this" is usually answerable with a usability session or a five-second test; "I want to know exactly what they see and in what order" is an eye tracking question.
