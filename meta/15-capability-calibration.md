# Capability Calibration Guide
**Type:** Meta / deployment reference | **Not for publication**
**Purpose:** Helps anyone deploying this content library — or embedded designers using it — calibrate what practitioners can self-serve vs. where a specialist role adds unique value. Also provides function-specific reading paths and a proactive design signal checklist.

---

## Section A — Self-service vs. specialist capability map

Design is embedded in platforms and squads (not centralized). The question is not "when to request design help" — it's "what does each role contribute that self-service tools can't?"

| Problem type | Self-service tools in this library | When a specialist adds unique value |
|---|---|---|
| Problem framing | 107, 108, 122 | **Strategist** — when framing spans org-level initiative scope, not team-level delivery |
| Lightweight research | 202, 208, 211 | **Researcher** — when a formal research plan, screened participants, or multi-round rigor is needed |
| Persona development | 301 arc | **Researcher** — when primary research is feasible and the persona will drive a major product direction |
| Journey mapping | 206 | **Researcher** — when current-state data is input (not assumption) and rigor of evidence matters |
| Visual / prototype | 106, 200 arc | **UX Designer** — when a production-quality deliverable is required |
| Facilitation | 304 arc | **UX Designer or Researcher** — when the embedded designer wants to run it rather than coach it |
| Measurement / OKRs | 305, 209, 210 | **Platform Excellence** — when platform-wide impact visibility or OKR-level reporting is involved |
| Strategic framing | 107, 104 | **Strategist** — when the initiative spans multiple teams or requires org-level stakeholder alignment |

## Section B — Function-specific reading paths

### Product Managers
Priority reading — addresses the documented PM myths about what design can and can't do:
1. **122** — Starting Questions (pre-project clarity ritual)
2. **107** — Framing the Problem (defines the problem before the solution)
3. **113** — Defining Success (builds a checkable outcome before work starts)
4. **202** — Lightweight Research Mechanics (data-driven, not opinion-driven)
5. **211** — Quick Comparative Scan (design does competitive analysis)
6. **207** — Premortem (addresses complex business problem risk before committing)
7. **301** arc — From a Vague Ask to a Real Persona (design works from evidence, not guesses)
8. **206** — Journey Mapping (design maps real workflows, not just UI)
9. **212** — Data Visualization for Decision-Makers (design uses and communicates data)
10. **209** — Design Decision Records (design documents rationale, not just outputs)
11. **303** arc — One Feature, Three Handoffs (design works across vendor and 3rd-party handoffs)
12. **304** arc — Running One Workshop (design facilitates, not just delivers)

## Section C — Proactive design signal checklist

Designers must be proactive — they don't wait to be requested. This checklist helps practitioners recognize inflection points where surfacing design input now (rather than later) prevents rework.

**Flag to your embedded designer when:**

| Signal | What it suggests | Relevant piece |
|---|---|---|
| A direction is being committed to and no one has written down what "worked" looks like | Definition-of-success gap | 113, 122 |
| A persona or user type is being referenced in decisions but hasn't been validated | Assumption risk in the decision layer | 301 arc |
| A prototype or feature is about to be demoed and no one has tested first-click or first-impression | Validation gap before a high-stakes moment | 208 |
| A feature is crossing a team boundary and each team has a different name for the same thing | Vocabulary/handoff breakdown risk | 303 arc |
| An AI workflow step is being built without testing what users do when it surprises them | Agent handoff blind spot | 302 arc |
| A retrospective is being held but no one defined success at the start | No valid check to run the retro against | 113, 210 |
| Something technically "works" and is moving to Hypercare but no one has mapped the support handoff | Post-MVP knowledge loss risk | 306, 305 |
| Work is restarting for the third time and no one has named what changed | Unclear problem or attachment to a failed direction | 100–103, 122 |
