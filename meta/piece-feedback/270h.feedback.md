## Synthesis — Revision Priorities

**Worth addressing when this piece comes up for revision:**
1. **Evaluation criteria should follow the question (strongest note).** Step 3 currently privileges fastest / fewest errors / least confusion. That quietly contradicts 177's "the prototype is defined by the question." Widening the criteria list to "whatever the question named in step 1 actually measures" is a consistency fix across the prototyping set, not a new method.
2. **Local vs. global maxima** restates the piece's existing "does this work vs. which works better" mechanism in optimization language. Useful if a sentence of prior-knowledge hook would help readers who already think in those terms; not worth a new named concept if it costs an experiential bridge. Don't add a hill-climbing lecture.
3. **Torres "compare and contrast vs. whether or not"** is the same mechanism the Dow/Tohidi sections already teach. Naming Torres may help readers who know her work and will cost readers who don't (see Magda on researcher-name handling, piece 103). Prefer using the distinction in plain language over adding a fourth named source unless the citation itself is load-bearing.

**Flagged but not actioned — single-source:**
4. Alberto's "add a concept that came to mind" pattern is adjacent to his worked-example requests on 105/147/177/219. Here the ask is conceptual vocabulary, not a story. Still one reviewer. Wait for a second read before making local/global maxima or Torres load-bearing.
