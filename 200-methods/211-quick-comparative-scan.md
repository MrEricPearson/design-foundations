# Quick Competitive/Comparative Scan
**Tier:** 200 — Practice | **Arc:** Standalone | **Prereqs:** None | **Note:** Prereq for 216 (Heuristic Evaluation — provides calibration context)

**Goal:** After this piece, you will be able to look at two or three existing solutions to a similar problem and produce honest notes before committing to your own direction.

**Prior knowledge hook:** Think of a feature or pattern you've built that felt obvious to you and your team — only to discover it existed in a dozen other products already, or the reverse: you built something novel that broke conventions users already knew and trusted.

**Trigger:** you're about to start building something without checking what already exists.

**Why this works:** comparing to existing solutions before committing identifies both the conventions users already know and the places where breaking convention is actually worth the cost. Without the comparison, you can't distinguish between the two.

**Method:** spend a short, fixed amount of time looking at two or three existing solutions to a similar problem. Note what works, what doesn't, and what you'd do differently.

**Artifact:** a few honest notes on existing approaches before you commit to your own.

**Watchout:** this is a quick gut-check, not a full audit — don't let it become an excuse to delay starting.

**Try This:** find one thing you're building that has a precedent somewhere. Look at two examples for 15 minutes. Write: one thing each does well, one thing each does poorly, and one thing you'd do differently. Time-box it.

**Proof:** Notes that include at least one thing you'd do differently from what you observed prove you looked honestly rather than to confirm what you already planned to build. Notes that only find things to criticize prove the opposite.

**Take this further:** in the next few days, share your comparison notes with one colleague. Write one sentence: what did they add or notice that you missed?

**After you've run this yourself:** describe what you're building and ask an AI tool to name three existing products or patterns that solve a similar problem — then look at those yourself and write the notes; the AI identifies the comparison set, you do the observation.

**What Next:** if the scan reveals usability patterns worth evaluating against — a common IA, a standard control — use those as calibration examples in 216 (Heuristic Evaluation). If you want to understand the mental models behind existing patterns before diverging from them, read 126 (Mental Models).
