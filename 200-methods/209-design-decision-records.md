# Design Decision Records
**Tier:** 200 — Practice | **Arc:** Standalone | **Prereqs:** 113 (defining success) | **Note:** Prereq for 210 (Retrospective), 303 (One Feature Three Handoffs), 305 (Did-It-Work Follow-Up)

**Goal:** After this piece, you will be able to write a short decision record at the moment a decision is made — preserving the reasoning before it gets reconstructed from memory.

**Prior knowledge hook:** Think of the last time a team decision got relitigated — someone said "I thought we decided X" and someone else said "no, we never settled that." The argument usually isn't about the decision itself; it's about whether it was ever actually made and what the reasoning was.

**Trigger:** a meaningful decision just got made, and there's a real risk it'll get relitigated later without anyone remembering why.

**Why this works:** a decision record written at the time of the decision contains the reasoning that was actually present then. The same reasoning reconstructed later contains what you remember being present — which is different. The timestamp is what makes the record checkable rather than revisable.

**Method:** write a short note — what was decided, why, and what alternatives were considered and rejected. Keep it wherever your team already keeps decisions.

**Artifact:** one short, dated record of a real decision and its reasoning.

**Watchout:** don't write one for every tiny choice — reserve this for decisions worth defending later, or it becomes noise nobody reads.

**Try This:** find the most recent meaningful decision in your current work — something that might get relitigated. Write: what was decided, why, and what alternatives were rejected. Date it. That's a decision record.

**Proof:** Six months after a decision, retrieve the record and try to answer "why did we decide this?" and "what would cause us to reverse it?" without talking to anyone who was there. If both questions are answerable from the document alone, the record is working. If you need to reconstruct — call someone, check Slack, guess — the record didn't capture what mattered. A record that can be read cold by someone who wasn't there and still makes the decision coherent is a complete record.

**Take this further:** in the next week, look at the decision record you wrote. Has it been challenged? Write one sentence: if a challenge came up, did the record hold — and what would you have added if you'd known the challenge was coming?

**What Next:** if you want to check whether a past decision actually held up, read 210 (Retrospective for Design Decisions). If this record needs to survive a team handoff, it belongs in the documentation for 303 (One Feature, Three Handoffs).
