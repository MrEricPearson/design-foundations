# Journey Mapping — Arc Overview and Approach Selection
**Tier:** Arc Header | **Arc:** Journey Mapping | **Prereq:** 175 (What a Journey Map Is) | **Note:** Read this before choosing an approach piece. Each approach piece is self-contained — if you already know your approach, go directly to it.

**Arc Goal:** After completing the relevant approach piece in this arc, you will be able to build a journey map that fits your actual constraints — whether you have user research, workshop access, or only your team's existing knowledge.

**Arc Trigger:** You need to understand a user's current experience to make a better decision, and you need to choose the approach that matches what you actually have access to.

---

## Choose Your Approach

The three journey mapping approaches differ in required access, required skills, and what kind of artifact they produce. They are not a progression — start with the one that fits your context.

---

### Approach C — Assumption-First Proto-Map → **206c**

**Use when:** You don't have access to users yet — or you're at a stage where documenting what your team currently believes is the right first step before investing in research or facilitation.

**What you bring:** What your team already knows. Prior conversations, support tickets, documentation, your own experience.

**What you produce:** A hypothesis map with explicit confidence markers. Each step is labeled "Seen," "Heard," or "Assuming." Pain points are labeled as hypothesized, not confirmed.

**The key judgment:** You are making team assumptions visible, not producing validated findings. The confidence markers are the method — without them, this produces false certainty rather than useful uncertainty.

**This is the right starting point** for most enterprise practitioners. If you're unsure which approach to use, start here.

→ **Read 206c (Assumption-First Proto-Map)**

---

### Approach A — Discovery-Driven Journey Map → **206a**

**Use when:** You have observations, interviews, or behavioral data to draw from — and your goal is to build a map grounded in what users actually do, not what your team believes they do.

**What you bring:** Research findings, interview notes, behavioral data, or direct observation records.

**What you produce:** A validated map with evidence citations at each step. Pain points are grounded in specific findings, not team inference.

**The key judgment:** The map is only as reliable as its evidence. Steps supported by weak evidence need confidence markers (same as Approach C) — the difference is that at least some steps have direct evidence behind them.

→ **Read 206a (Discovery-Driven Journey Map)**

---

### Approach B — Co-Creative Journey Map → **206b**

**Use when:** Alignment and shared ownership are goals alongside documentation — you need the people in the room to feel that the map belongs to them, not just to the design team.

**What you bring:** Facilitation skills, a room with the right participants (users and/or stakeholders), and a clear scope.

**What you produce:** A shared-ownership map built collectively. The process of building it is part of the value — the artifact carries the room's knowledge and buy-in simultaneously.

**The key judgment:** Workshop-generated maps inherit the room's biases. A room that shares the same blindspot produces a polished map of that blindspot. The map should be labeled as a collective mental model, not a validated finding, unless it's explicitly grounded in user-provided evidence during the session.

→ **Read 206b (Co-Creative Journey Map)**

## Enterprise Scale: When to Add Swimlanes

When your experience involves more than one distinct persona or role, and their steps meaningfully differ, a single-lane map misrepresents the system. Add swimlanes — one horizontal track per persona — so the comparison is explicit.

The decision point: if two personas go through the same steps but feel differently about them, that difference lives in annotations on one lane. If the steps themselves differ — different touchpoints, different sequences, different drop-off points — they need separate lanes.

Read 176 (Swimlanes as a Comparison Tool) before adding lanes to any approach.
