# Grooming with AI
**Tier:** 200 — Practice | **Arc:** Full arc (3 parts) | **Prereqs:** 109 (weighing tradeoffs), 111 (self-critique); pairs with 200 (Story Mapping), 147 (AI as Execution Partner)

**Goal:** After this arc, you will be able to use AI to prepare a grooming-ready backlog — sized stories with acceptance criteria — that survives a real scrutiny session.

**Prior knowledge hook:** Think of the last grooming session that went over time, or ended with stories that weren't ready — criteria missing, scope unclear, the room uncertain what "done" would look like. That's what this addresses.

**Trigger:** you're preparing for a grooming session and want to move faster without sacrificing quality.

**Why this works:** AI can draft criteria and size stories quickly from a description — but it can only work with what you give it. Using AI as a drafting tool forces you to describe your work precisely enough for someone who knows nothing about it to understand it. That precision is the real grooming preparation, not the output AI produces.

---

## Part 1: Right-Sizing Stories

A story that's too big hides risk inside it. AI can help break work down quickly — but the sizing judgment is still yours.

**Method:**
1. Take a slice from your story map and ask an AI assistant to break it into smaller, right-sized stories.
2. Review each suggested story: is it small enough to finish and test in a short cycle?
3. Split or merge as needed — the AI's first pass is a draft, not the final answer.

**What you end up with:** a set of stories sized for actual delivery, not just plausible-sounding chunks.

**Proof:** A right-sized story is one you can answer "how will we know this is done?" for without hesitation. If an AI-suggested story can't pass that check, it's either too large or missing context — and the check surfaces that before the session, not during it.

**Watchout:** AI will happily produce plausible-sounding stories that hide missing context. You still own the judgment call on whether they're actually right-sized.

---

## Part 2: Writing Acceptance Criteria With AI

Acceptance criteria define "done." AI can draft them fast — but drafted doesn't mean correct.

**Method:**
1. Ask the AI to draft acceptance criteria for a story, based on what you know so far.
2. Read each criterion and ask: do I actually understand what this means, and do I agree it's the right bar?
3. Edit or remove anything you can't fully stand behind.

**What you end up with:** acceptance criteria you genuinely understand and agree with, not just criteria that sound thorough.

**Proof:** The test is whether you can explain each criterion in your own words without looking at the AI's version. Criteria you can explain are yours now. Criteria you can't aren't ready — not because the AI drafted them, but because they haven't been understood yet.

**Watchout:** don't accept criteria you don't fully understand just because they read well. If you can't explain it in your own words, it's not ready.

---

## Part 3: Running the Grooming Pass

The actual grooming conversation is still where the real value happens — AI speeds up prep, it doesn't replace the discussion.

**Method:**
1. Bring your AI-assisted draft (stories + criteria) into the grooming session as a starting point, not a finished product.
2. Explicitly invite disagreement — ask the room what's missing or wrong, not just for approval.
3. Update the stories based on what comes up.

**What you end up with:** a groomed backlog that's been through real scrutiny, not just an AI-generated draft nobody questioned.

**Proof:** A session that produces at least one update to the AI-drafted material is working. One where the draft sails through unchanged either had an unusually clean draft or — more likely — nobody felt safe disagreeing. The update is the signal, not the session completing.

**Watchout:** a fast AI-assisted pass can create false confidence. Leave room in the session for the room to actually disagree.

**Try This:** take one story you're preparing for grooming. Ask an AI tool to draft three acceptance criteria for it. Check each: can you explain it in your own words? Edit any you can't.

**Take this further:** in the next sprint, after grooming closes, check one story that went through this process against what was actually delivered. Write one sentence: did the acceptance criteria match what "done" meant when you shipped? The gap (or lack of one) is the feedback on your grooming quality.

**What Next:** once the backlog is groomed, the story map that produced it is the design artifact to reference back when scope questions arise. Keep both in your discovery space (121).
